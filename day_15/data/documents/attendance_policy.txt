Attendance Policy

Employees are expected to work the schedule agreed with their manager and to be ready to begin work at the scheduled start time. The attendance policy applies to office, remote, and hybrid work. A team may use a shared calendar, timekeeping tool, or approved attendance system to record working hours. The tool used by a team does not change the expectation that records are accurate.

If an employee will be late, absent, or unable to work the planned schedule, they should notify their manager before the shift begins whenever possible. The message should state the expected arrival time or the length of the absence and should use the team's approved communication channel. An employee should not rely on a coworker to report an absence unless an emergency makes direct contact impossible.

Repeated lateness, unexplained absences, or inaccurate time records may require a conversation with the manager and HR. The purpose of an initial conversation is to understand the cause, clarify expectations, and agree on a practical improvement plan. Serious or repeated violations may lead to formal action under the company's conduct process. The company considers approved leave, disability-related needs, emergencies, and other protected circumstances according to applicable law and internal procedures.

Remote employees should keep their work calendar current, attend required meetings, and remain reachable during agreed working hours. A temporary connectivity problem should be reported promptly with an alternate contact method when available. Employees should record overtime or schedule changes using the process defined by their team; they should not silently work hours that create an inaccurate record.

Managers are responsible for applying the policy consistently, recording relevant facts, and involving HR when a pattern needs review. They should not diagnose a medical issue or ask for unnecessary personal information. Employees who believe an attendance record is wrong should raise it with the manager or HR. Questions about schedules, flexible working arrangements, and adjustments should be discussed before a problem becomes a repeated pattern.
